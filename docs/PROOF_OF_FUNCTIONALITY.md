## 2. Algorithms and limitations

See `docs/SCENARIO_METHOD.md`. In short: a Haversine straight-line distance to the nearest active facility for 72 grid points; summaries before and after hypothetical facilities; the canal-belt subset; site screening over a 100 m candidate grid. **Limitations:** not walking distance; not population; OSM completeness and access are unverified; no risk prediction.

## 4. Two locations, two different results (real data)

From `docs/PROOF_RUN_OUTPUT.md` (swimming, core scope, 1 km threshold):

| Scenario | Points ≤ 1 km | Mean | Median | Max | Points improved | Canal belt ≤ 1 km |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | 13 / 72 | 1,99 km | 1,97 km | 4,21 km | 0 | 3 / 27 |
| **A:** 37.0105, 35.3205 (east, Gazipaşa) | **27 / 72** | 1,23 km | 1,23 km | 2,69 km | 32 | 12 / 27 |
| **B:** 37.008, 35.285 (west, İsmetpaşa) | **21 / 72** | 1,70 km | 1,48 km | 4,11 km | 35 | 7 / 27 |

Why A beats B on this metric: A sits about 2.8 km (2,846 m) from the nearest core pool, so it fills the eastern gap where the five mapped pools (all in the north-west) are furthest away. B shortens distances for more points (35), but many of them stay above 1 km. The per-point table in `PROOF_RUN_OUTPUT.md` shows each change.

Sensitivity: with unnamed pools included (scope = all), the baseline jumps to **44 / 72**. The result depends heavily on which records are trusted, which is why the scope switch is shown in the UI.

Development note: before the 100 m candidate grid was added, the “best” button only tested the 72 sample points. In one E2E run a hand-moved location (+12) beat it (+10). We then switched to the 100 m grid (`ENGINEERING_DECISIONS.md` #10).

## 5. Evidence of dynamic recalculation

- `evaluateScenario` runs inside `useMemo` keyed on hypothetical positions (`KanalAtlasApp.tsx`). Leaflet's `drag` event updates the position on every frame. A test fails if the headline or coordinates do not change after moving.
- The demo video (`submission/video/kanalatlas-demo-draft.webm`, recorded from the live production build by `npm run demo:record`) shows the counts and amber lines changing continuously while the marker is dragged. The recording script **throws an error if the drag does not move the marker**, and its captions read the numbers from the live DOM rather than hard-coding them.
